The Examined Society

Markets, morals, justice, and knowledge: a year-long argument about how we live together.

“The unexamined life is not worth living.”— Socrates, in Plato’s Apology

The idea

One question, many traditions

How can people trade, argue, judge, and decide together? The Examined Society brings economics into conversation with political philosophy, moral philosophy, and the theory of knowledge.

We read thinkers who disagree deeply, treating their arguments seriously and leaving room for participants to make their own connections. The aim is to weigh rival ideas with care, see the trade-offs they reveal, and resist easy answers.

“No tradition is granted the last word.”

From Hume’s account of justice to contemporary questions about democracy, climate, and artificial intelligence, the sequence follows an argument rather than a single doctrine.

The syllabus

A 34-meeting conversation

The route moves from moral sentiments and commercial society through critiques of capitalism, collective choice, justice, and the growth of knowledge. The final meeting brings the whole conversation to bear on seven current strains.

Before each meeting

A guide and an original voice

For meetings 1–33, participants receive a concise note on the thinker and a short excerpt from the original writing. Read them side by side: the note offers orientation; the excerpt lets the author speak. Together they will not exceed 50–60 pages. Meeting 34 uses a longer synthesis note. Read the full syllabus →
